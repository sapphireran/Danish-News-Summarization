# Hjelmøerne

A fictional island chain invented for this workbook so the examples can
be public. Nothing here is a real newsroom, a real ferry company, or a
row from the 2023 dump.

## Geography

Five named islands and a handful of inland places:

- **Hjelmhøj** — largest town, brass band, theatre guild, school
- **Klintø** — chess club, knitting guild, customs house
- **Saltøre** — cargo-bike co-op, letterpress shop
- **Mågeodde** — seaweed racks, tide pool, boatshed cinema overflow
- **Ravnskær** — film guild, hand-crank projector
- **Sognehavn** — bakery, reading house, fishers
- **Grådyb** — the water, and the ferry company named after it
- **Æblehaven**, **Tørveengen**, **Sandvig**, **Vesterklit**,
  **Fogedtoften**, **Lynghuset**, **Færgelejet**, **Broagerstien**

## The magazine

*Kystlinje* is a quarterly that writes about kiln firings, dialect
tapes, weather vanes, and night boats. The register is coastal cultural
reporting, not city-hall minutes. That is deliberate: other personal
branches already used municipal-news fiction. This world is a zine.

## People who recur

Signe Brix and Kaj Nygaard play chess. Petra Skov chairs the chess club
and talks about knitting slips. Lærke Holm records speakers over eighty.
Otto Kvist cuts glass. Ida Fjaltring grafts apples and wades tide
pools. Ellen Krag counts hives. Viggo Tranberg tunes pianos and runs
the last letterpress. Bo Hjelm conducts and comments on ferry times.
Mira Holst writes the island novel the reading house stays up for.

Recurring names are a feature. They test whether the ledger can track
the same person across briefs without confusing `Hjelmhøj` the place
with `Hjelmhøj Messingorkester` the band.
